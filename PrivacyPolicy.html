Privacy Policy

How the LingoShare iOS app handles information. Published by Farhan Maqsood, an individual developer.

1. Who this policy covers

LingoShare is an iOS app published by Farhan Maqsood, an individual developer. This policy describes the current app. It applies to LingoShare on iPhone and iPad, including the Share Extension.

These pages are published at https://farhan-maqsood.github.io/LingoShare-Legals/PrivacyPolicy.html. The app’s copyright line is “© 2026 Farhan Maqsood”.

This policy does not cover apps, websites, or services you open from LingoShare, such as Mail, Files, Maps, Calendar, Reminders, Phone, AirPlay, or a website you choose to download from. Those services apply their own terms.

Privacy questions go to the developer at purenesttech@gmail.com.

2. What the app does

LingoShare captures a page and reads and translates the text on the device. From a document you can also use:

  • Key Facts — dates, amounts, phone numbers, addresses, and links found in the text on the device
  • Bilingual handout — a PDF that places the page with its on-device translation
  • Big screen — the page you select, sent to a display you pick with AirPlay or a cable
  • Phone nearby — send or receive files with another device on the same Wi-Fi, using local, unencrypted HTTP

The app can also import photos and files, keep a local library, and offer tools such as signatures, stamps, and PDF export. There is no LingoShare account.

3. Summary

  • Farhan Maqsood does not operate a cloud library of your pages. Scans, OCR text, and translations are stored on the device unless you choose to move them.
  • Reading and translation are designed to run on the device after any needed language pack is already there. Adding a language downloads that pack over the network. Downloads may use cellular data.
  • The app includes Google ML Kit and GoogleDataTransport. Google’s published iOS disclosure says those libraries collect device and app information, diagnostics, and usage analytics. That disclosure is separate from uploading your page images to the developer. See ML Kit’s iOS data disclosure.
  • A page leaves the device when you export, share, print, download from a link, open Key Facts actions, present on a Big screen, or use Phone nearby.
  • Phone nearby is ordinary HTTP on the local network. It is not encrypted. Anyone on that Wi-Fi who can see the traffic can read the transfer.
  • Subscriptions are sold by Apple. The developer does not receive your card number.

4. Information the developer does not take as an account or a document library

The current app does not ask you to register an account with the developer. It does not upload your scanned pages to a server operated by Farhan Maqsood for storage or processing.

The current app does not include a third-party advertising SDK, and the app source does not request App Tracking Transparency. Support email is something you send. The app does not collect your name, email address, or phone number into an account.

5. Information stored on your device

LingoShare stores information locally so you can capture, read, translate, and organize pages. Depending on what you use, that can include:

  • Scanned and imported page images, cropped or edited images, and generated PDFs, including bilingual handouts
  • Folders, titles, and document metadata
  • OCR text and translations you generate
  • Signatures, stamps, watermarks, and annotations you add
  • App preferences, such as appearance, language choices, free-use counters, and a local cache of subscription status
  • Privacy Lock settings, if you turn that feature on
  • Downloaded on-device language and ML model files
  • Temporary files for the Share Extension, in the app group group.fm.share.DocNest, and temporary files for Phone nearby

If you turn on Privacy Lock, the PIN is written to the device Keychain with accessibility “when unlocked, this device only,” after the app checks that the write can be read back. An item stored that way is not restored onto a different device by an iOS backup. If the Keychain write fails, the app can keep that PIN in on-device preferences so an existing lock is not lost. The PIN is not sent to the developer. Face ID or Touch ID, if you enable it, is handled by the device. The developer does not receive biometric samples.

You can import a file from the Files app, including a file stored in iCloud Drive. The system document picker brings a copy onto the device. The current Settings screen does not offer a working control that syncs the LingoShare library to iCloud. The app’s entitlements still include the iCloud Documents container iCloud.com.fm.DocNest. This policy does not claim that container is unused.

6. Permissions

LingoShare asks for access when a feature needs it:

  • Camera — capture pages for reading and translation, scan a QR code for Phone nearby, and use ID or passport tools
  • Photo library — choose photos to read, and save an image when you pick Save Image in the share sheet
  • Files — import or export documents you choose, including files in iCloud Drive
  • Local network and Bonjour (_docnestxfer._tcp) — Phone nearby on the same Wi-Fi
  • Face ID or Touch ID — unlock Privacy Lock
  • Calendar — only if you choose to add a date from Key Facts. On iOS 17 and later the app asks for write-only Calendar access for that save
  • Reminders — only if you choose to save a Key Facts date as a reminder. That request is for Reminders access, described in the system prompt as full access on current iOS versions

7. On-device reading, translation, and language downloads

OCR and translation are intended to run on the device. The app uses Apple frameworks and Google ML Kit for text recognition (including Chinese, Devanagari, Japanese, and Korean script models), language identification, and translation.

English translation does not require a downloaded pack in the current translation code. Other languages use a pack downloaded by ML Kit’s model manager. Those downloads allow cellular access and background downloading. After the pack is on the device, translation of text you choose is designed to run offline. Cancelling in the app marks the download cancelled for the interface; the ML Kit transfer may continue in the background.

8. Google ML Kit and GoogleDataTransport

The app links Google ML Kit and related Google libraries, including GoogleDataTransport. The locked dependency versions in this project are Google ML Kit 9.0.0 (text recognition, translation, and language identification), ML Kit Translate 8.0.0, ML Kit Common 14.0.0, and GoogleDataTransport 10.1.0.

Google’s page Prepare for Apple’s App Store data disclosure requirements (page date 10 July 2024 UTC) says it lists data collected by the latest version of each ML Kit iOS SDK. For all features, that page says the SDK collects, by default, for diagnostics and usage analytics:

  • Device information, such as manufacturer, model, OS version and build, and available ML hardware accelerators
  • Application information, including bundle ID and app versions
  • Per-installation identifiers that Google says are not intended to uniquely identify a user or physical device
  • Performance metrics, such as latency
  • API configuration, such as image format and resolution
  • Event types, such as feature initialization, model download, detection, and resource release
  • Error codes for those events

For translation, the same page says ML Kit collects the configured source and destination languages for diagnostics and usage analytics, and that ML Kit uses Firebase Remote Config and Firebase Installations for remote-configuration diagnostics. For Language ID, the page says identified languages are used for diagnostics and usage analytics. This project’s Podfile does not declare separate Firebase pods. Whether those Firebase paths run inside the linked ML Kit binaries has not been confirmed by observing network traffic from this app.

Privacy manifests shipped inside the installed ML Kit pods declare categories that include Device ID, Other Diagnostic Data, Other User Content, Other Data Types, Performance Data, and Product Interaction. The purposes listed there are Analytics, App Functionality, or both. The GoogleDataTransport 10.1.0 manifest declares Other Diagnostic Data for Analytics. Those manifests mark the categories as not linked to the user and not used for tracking, or they omit a tracking flag. A manifest is the library author’s declaration. It is not a recording of what this build sent.

Google’s page describes the latest SDKs, and it is not a measurement of LingoShare traffic. Do not read this section as a statement that every listed field leaves the device on every launch, or as a statement that page images are uploaded to the developer. The developer does not use these libraries to show third-party ads.

9. When you send content somewhere else

  • Share, export, print, AirDrop, or Mail — the file goes to the destination you pick. On the free plan, shared and printed pages include a small LingoShare mark. LingoShare Plus can turn that mark off.
  • Phone nearby — described in the next section. The developer’s servers are not on that path.
  • Big screen — the selected page is shown on the AirPlay display, Apple TV, Mac, or cabled screen you connect. The developer does not host that presentation. People who can see the screen can see the page.
  • Download from a link — you enter an http or https address, and the app fetches that file from the host in the link. The developer does not operate that host.
  • Key Facts actions — Calendar, Reminders, Maps, Phone, or a link open only when you tap them. See section 11.
  • In-app purchases — processed by Apple. See section 12.
  • Privacy Policy and Terms — the app opens these pages in Safari.

10. Phone nearby

Phone nearby lets you send or receive documents with a phone or computer on the same Wi-Fi. The current build discovers the session with Bonjour service _docnestxfer._tcp and transfers bytes with cleartext HTTP on the local network. The listener tries TCP ports 8080 through 8090. This is not TLS. App Transport Security in the app allows local networking.

You start the session. The host shows a 6-digit PIN. The QR code and copied link are an http:// address with the host and port. The PIN is not placed in that URL. After the PIN is accepted, later requests use a session token. Stopping the session, leaving the screen, or letting the session expire ends that sharing session.

Because the transfer is unencrypted, a person or device on the same Wi-Fi who can observe the traffic can read it. Use a network you trust when the pages are sensitive. The developer does not receive the files from Phone nearby and does not relay them through a LingoShare server.

11. Key Facts, Calendar, and Reminders

Key Facts looks on the device for dates, amounts, phone numbers, addresses, and links in text already read from the document. Nothing in that list is sent to the developer by the extraction itself.

If you tap a date, the app can add a Calendar event or a Reminder. The title and notes can include text from the document, the document title, and the page number. Calendar and Reminders then hold that item under your device accounts. If those accounts sync, Apple or the account provider handles the copy under their own policies. The developer does not receive the event.

If you tap a phone number, the app opens a tel: link. If you tap an address, the app opens Apple Maps with that address as the query (https://maps.apple.com/). If you tap a link found in the document, the app opens that URL. Each of those happens only after you tap.

12. Purchases

LingoShare Plus is sold through Apple’s StoreKit and the App Store. The product identifiers in the app are com.fm.DocNest.Weekly, com.fm.DocNest.Monthly, and com.fm.DocNest.Yearly. Apple shows the price and renewal terms before you buy. Apple processes payment under Apple’s privacy policy and App Store terms.

The developer does not receive your payment card number or Apple ID password. The app keeps a local cache of entitlement state so Plus features can be recognized on the device, including when StoreKit is briefly unavailable.

13. How long information stays, and backups

  • Document files and settings remain until you delete them in the app, or until you remove the app.
  • Deleting a document removes it from the app’s local library paths for that item. Copies you already exported, printed, transferred, or presented are outside the app.
  • Deleting the app removes the app’s local container, subject to copies you exported and to device or iCloud backups.
  • A search of the app’s Swift source found no isExcludedFromBackup flag on the document library. iOS backups can include app data under your device settings. A Privacy Lock PIN stored as “this device only” is not restored onto another device. A PIN that fell back to on-device preferences can be included in a backup.

14. Children

LingoShare does not have user accounts, so there is no account a child can register with the developer. The app is not designed to collect personal information from children for the developer. If you believe a child sent personal information to the support email, write to purenesttech@gmail.com.

15. Security

Page reading and translation are designed to stay on the device once language resources are present, and the developer does not operate a document cloud for this app. On-device storage can still be lost, copied in a backup, or exposed if the device is unlocked. Phone nearby is visible to observers on the same Wi-Fi because it is unencrypted HTTP. A device passcode, Privacy Lock, and care about what you share or present reduce that exposure. They do not make it zero.

This policy does not describe LingoShare as fully private, and it does not say that nothing ever leaves the device.
